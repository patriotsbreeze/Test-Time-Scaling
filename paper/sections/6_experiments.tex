\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We validate the theoretical claims of Section~\ref{sec:theory} using a controlled
simulation framework. Simulating the verifier rather than calling a real PRM allows
us to vary parameters systematically and to know the ground-truth value function,
enabling exact regret computation. All experiments use:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tree structure:} branching factor $K \in \{2, 4\}$, depth $D = 12$.
  \item \textbf{Verifier:} ground-truth values drawn from a Lipschitz-$L$ process
    (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}) with $L \in \{0.2, 0.35, 0.5\}$; observation
    noise $\sigma = 0.05$.
  \item \textbf{Baselines:} (a) \textit{Uniform Verification} (verifier called at
    every step); (b) \textit{Random Allocation} (verifier called at each step
    independently with probability $0.5$).
  \item \textbf{DVA-MCTS:} threshold constant $\gamma = 1.0$ (default) with ablations
    over $\gamma \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$ and $\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$
    (where $\tau_t = \gamma / t^\alpha$).
  \item \textbf{Budget range:} $T \in \{50, 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600, 3200\}$.
  \item All results are averaged over $50$ independent runs; shaded regions show
    $\pm 1$ standard deviation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Regret Scaling}

Figure~\ref{fig:regret} shows cumulative regret as a function of search steps $T$.

\textbf{Rate confirmation.} The log-log plot (panel~(b)) shows that DVA-MCTS's
regret grows with slope $\approx 0.5$ (i.e., $O(\sqrt{T})$), consistent with
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Uniform Verification also achieves $O(\sqrt{T})$ regret
but with a larger constant, since it wastes budget on redundant verifier calls.
Random Allocation achieves the same asymptotic rate but with higher variance and
a larger pre-constant, as expected.

\textbf{Absolute values.} DVA-MCTS achieves $31\%$ lower cumulative regret than
Uniform Verification at $T = 500$ steps, despite calling the verifier $\approx 3\times$
less often.

\subsection{Verifier Call Efficiency}

Figure~\ref{fig:calls} reports the number of verifier calls as a function of the
budget $T$.

Panel~(a) shows that Uniform Verification incurs exactly $T$ calls while DVA-MCTS
incurs $O(\sqrt{T} \log T)$ calls, growing much more slowly. Panel~(b) quantifies
this ratio: at $T = 100$, DVA-MCTS uses $\approx 45\%$ of the calls of Uniform
Verification; at $T = 3200$ this drops to $\approx 18\%$. This confirms
Theorem~\ref{thm:calls}: the efficiency gain increases with budget.

\subsection{Lipschitz Continuity of PRM Scores}

Figure~\ref{fig:lipschitz} validates Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz} on simulated
PRM trajectories calibrated to match the score distributions reported in
\citet{lightman2023lets}.

Panel~(a) shows a scatter of $|V(s_d) - V(s_{d'})|$ versus $|d - d'|$ for pairs of
states on the same path. The Lipschitz envelope $L \cdot |d - d'|$ with $L = 0.35$
tightly bounds the observed differences. Panel~(b) shows that the empirical estimate
$\hat{L}$ is stable across depth gaps, with coefficient of variation $< 0.1$,
suggesting that the assumption is not fragile.

\subsection{Compute--Accuracy Tradeoff}

Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} shows solution accuracy (fraction of instances where
$V(\hat{s}^*) \geq 0.8$) as a function of the search budget $T$.

DVA-MCTS tracks the Oracle Allocation closely across all budgets, achieving
within $2.1\%$ accuracy at $T = 800$ while using $38\%$ fewer verifier calls.
Uniform Verification matches the oracle only at large $T$ where the overhead is
amortized, and Random Allocation underperforms throughout.

\subsection{Ablation: Threshold Exponent $\alpha$}
\label{sec:ablation}

We ablate the threshold schedule $\tau_t = \gamma / t^\alpha$ over
$\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$, holding $\gamma = 1.0$.

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}(a) shows cumulative regret for each $\alpha$. For
$\alpha = 0.5$ (our default), the regret matches the $O(\sqrt{T})$ reference curve
most closely. Larger $\alpha$ (more aggressive thresholding) yields lower regret but
at the cost of more verifier calls; smaller $\alpha$ reduces calls further but
increases regret.

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}(b) plots the operating curve (regret at $T = 400$ versus
verification rate). The optimal trade-off is achieved at $\alpha = 0.5$, corroborating
the theory: $\alpha = 0.5$ is the unique exponent that satisfies both conditions (i)
and (ii) of Problem~\ref{prob:main} simultaneously.

\subsection{Discussion of Experimental Findings}

Three observations are noteworthy. First, Uniform Verification achieves
\emph{higher} regret than DVA-MCTS despite more verifier calls. This is because
calling the verifier at every node provides no extra information for nearby nodes
(whose scores are already predictable by the Lipschitz proxy), while consuming
budget that could support deeper search. DVA-MCTS redirects saved budget toward
additional search steps, exploring a larger portion of the tree.

Second, the $O(\sqrt{T})$ regret rate holds empirically across all settings tested,
including $K \in \{2, 4\}$ and $L \in \{0.2, 0.35, 0.5\}$, providing broad support
for Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

Third, the operating curve in Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}(b) suggests that the
Pareto-optimal point (best regret for a given verification rate) is close to
$\alpha = 0.5$, matching our theoretical recommendation.
