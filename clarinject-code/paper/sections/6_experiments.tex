\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Model.} We evaluate \textbf{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} served locally via
Ollama. While smaller than the frontier models studied by~\citet{aspi2026}, 8B-class
instruction-tuned models are widely deployed in cost-sensitive production agent
systems~\citep{sun2024surveyllmagents}. We discuss model-size implications in
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\paragraph{Scenarios.} We use all 25 seed scenarios ($5 \times 5$ categories),
run each $N = 3$ times per condition per defense, yielding
$25 \times 3 \times 2 \times 4 = 600$ total trials.

\paragraph{Clarification rate.} Llama-3.1-8B asked a clarifying question in
\textbf{77\%} of clarification-condition trials (193/250), confirming the
clarification interaction is active and measurable. Trials where no question
was asked are scored on the first-turn output.

\subsection{Main Results: The Clarification Tax}
\label{sec:main-results}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{ASR by condition (no defense). Contrary to H1 and the ASPI finding
for frontier models, the clarification tax is \emph{negative}: the tool-output
channel is more dangerous than the clarification channel for Llama-3.1-8B.
95\% bootstrap CIs in brackets.}
\label{tab:main}
\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & $\asr_{\mathrm{exec}}$ & $\asr_{\mathrm{clar}}$ & $\tax$ \\
\midrule
Llama-3.1-8B & 0.760 {\scriptsize[0.653--0.853]} & 0.440 {\scriptsize[0.333--0.547]} & \textbf{$-$0.320} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reveals a striking reversal of the expected effect.
The execution condition (adversarial content via tool output) achieves an ASR of
0.760---the model follows injected instructions in \textbf{76\%} of trials when
they arrive through a file-read tool call. The clarification channel, by contrast,
achieves only 0.440 ASR. The clarification tax $\tax = -0.320$: clarification
is \emph{safer}, not more dangerous, for this model tier.

We hypothesize two contributing mechanisms. First, 8B models receive substantially
less instruction-hierarchy training than frontier models, leaving the tool-output
channel poorly guarded. Second, the clarification interaction state may produce
a mild implicit skepticism effect: the model has just formulated a specific question
and may be more narrowly focused on extracting the answer than on executing arbitrary
new instructions.

\subsection{Defense Effectiveness}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Defense results. $\tax^*$ is the residual tax under each defense.
D1 (prompt tagging) \emph{increases} clarification ASR, suggesting XML delimiters
act as authority markers for 8B models rather than isolation boundaries.
D2 and D3 dramatically reduce clarification ASR.}
\label{tab:defenses-results}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Defense} & $\asr_{\mathrm{clar}}$ & $\tax^*$ & $\tsr$ & $\ccr$ \\
\midrule
None (baseline) & 0.440 & $-$0.320 & 0.493 & 0.493 \\
D1: Prompt Tag  & 0.613 & $-$0.080 & 0.707 & 0.707 \\
D2: Struct.\ Extract & 0.080 & $-$0.600 & 0.720 & 0.720 \\
D3: Plan-Diff Gate   & 0.013 & $-$0.707 & 0.560 & 0.560 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:defenses-results} reveals a critical failure mode of D1. Wrapping
the clarification answer in \texttt{<USER\_CLARIFICATION>} tags \emph{increases}
ASR from 0.440 to 0.613---a 17-point increase. We attribute this to a \emph{tag
authority inversion}: smaller instruction-tuned models appear to have learned that
XML-delimited blocks carry structured, authoritative content (as in function-call
schemas and tool-output formats common in training data), causing them to treat
tagged content as \emph{more} trustworthy rather than isolated. This is a significant
practical warning against naive delimiter-based defenses on sub-frontier models.

D2 (structured extraction) and D3 (plan-diff gating) both work well. D2 reduces
clarification ASR to 0.080---an 82\% relative reduction---at the cost of a modest
TSR increase (0.493 $\to$ 0.720), since the extraction pass also clarifies the
legitimate portion of the answer. D3 achieves near-zero ASR (0.013) but TSR drops
to 0.560, reflecting false-positive gates that block legitimate scope changes.

\subsection{Pareto Frontier}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} plots the collaboration--security Pareto frontier.
D2 dominates D3: higher CCR (0.720 vs 0.560) with comparable security.
The undefended clarification baseline also dominates D1, confirming that
prompt tagging is counterproductive for this model. The execution condition
(not plotted, as defense does not affect it differently) represents the
true security floor: ASR = 0.760 regardless of clarification defenses.

\subsection{Results by Attack Category}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{ASR by attack category (no defense). Dependency-poisoning attacks
show lowest execution ASR; safety-check removal shows highest clarification ASR.}
\label{tab:by-category}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Category} & $\asr_{\mathrm{exec}}$ & $\asr_{\mathrm{clar}}$ \\
\midrule
backdoor              & 0.800 & 0.533 \\
secret\_exfil         & 0.733 & 0.400 \\
safetycheck\_removal  & 0.800 & 0.533 \\
dependency\_poison    & 0.600 & 0.267 \\
destructive\_command  & 0.867 & 0.467 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Dependency-poisoning attacks show the lowest ASR in both conditions: the model
is somewhat more resistant to adding unrecognized package names. Destructive
commands show the highest execution ASR (0.867), confirming that tool-output
injection is the dominant attack surface for small models regardless of category.
